## 2. Where the extra electricity comes from (exact decomposition)

The controller changes only the heat that passes through the tanks (heat pump to tank, tank to demand); the demand is fixed. So the extra heat-pump electricity is exactly: **heat lost in the tanks** (converted at the charging COP) + **heat still stored at the end of the year** + **COP timing** (charging when it is colder than when the heat is used, or the reverse).

## 4. What the model supports, and what it does not

**Supported by the accounting (given the assumptions in section 6):**
- *Night-charging rule*: cost +180 CHF. Shifting to cheaper hours saved 209 CHF, but 2 577 kWh_el of extra electricity cost 389 CHF; 73 % of that extra electricity is tank losses and 25 % is COP timing. The tariff gain does **not** cover the extra electricity.
- *Tariff-timed rule (gentle)*: cost +168 CHF. Shifting to cheaper hours saved 167 CHF, but 2 225 kWh_el of extra electricity cost 335 CHF; 78 % of that extra electricity is tank losses and 20 % is COP timing. The tariff gain does **not** cover the extra electricity.
- *Tariff-timed rule (medium)*: cost +141 CHF. Shifting to cheaper hours saved 233 CHF, but 2 485 kWh_el of extra electricity cost 374 CHF; 75 % of that extra electricity is tank losses and 23 % is COP timing. The tariff gain does **not** cover the extra electricity.
- *Tariff-timed rule (strong)*: cost +103 CHF. Shifting to cheaper hours saved 276 CHF, but 2 518 kWh_el of extra electricity cost 379 CHF; 76 % of that extra electricity is tank losses and 22 % is COP timing. The tariff gain does **not** cover the extra electricity.

**Not supported or only partly supported:**
- *Peak demand.* The highest single hour changes by -6.1 % (Night-charging rule), -3.8 % (Tariff-timed rule (gentle)), -5.2 % (Tariff-timed rule (medium)), +1.2 % (Tariff-timed rule (strong)), but the mean daily peak changes by only -0.35 %, -0.05 %, -0.04 %, +0.02 %. A claim that a rule "reduces peaks" holds at most for the single annual maximum, not for typical days; Tariff-timed rule (strong) even raises the annual maximum.
- *Carbon.* The carbon-timing term is positive for 4 of 4 rules: the cheap hours are not the clean hours in the modeled series. The level of that series is 61-63 % of the EPFL reference (D-013b), so only the sign and rough size of relative changes may be quoted, never tonnes. Carbon reductions are not a finding of Phase A.
- *Why the controller charges when it does* is a property of the rule (clock or tariff map), not something the simulation explains; the check covers accounting, not intent.
- *Comfort and unmet heat* are not modeled; demand is always met, so no statement about comfort can be made.

## 5. Two aggregations that must not be mixed

The CityLearn KPI table (`kpi_*` columns) is the **unweighted mean of the six per-building ratios** (verified: the mean of the per-building carbon, cost and import ratios of the night-charging rule reproduces its KPI values to 1e-5, `test_per_building_kpi_average_differs_from_district_total`). The district totals above are energy-weighted. They answer different questions and differ in size (for the night-charging rule the carbon KPI is 1.0236, the district total 1.0267). Use district totals for district claims and label the KPI table as an average of building ratios.

## 6. What these conclusions depend on

- Tank sizes, efficiency (0.95) and standby loss (0.3 %/h) are **assumed** (D-017a, ISS-029, ISS-032). The tank-loss share above is a result of those assumptions.
- Heat demand is derived from electricity and its timing is unvalidated (ISS-028, ISS-030); the COP form is a Carnot-fraction model (M-001).
- One year (2023), one tariff (EKZ standard, excl. VAT), carbon modeled and relative only.
- Sensitivity (night-charging rule, 4 variants, not including the base case): cost change +0.22 % to +1.10 %, peak -6.7 % to -3.1 % (`sensitivity_2023.csv`).
- Sensitivity (tariff-timed rule (medium), 4 variants, not including the base case): cost change +0.15 % to +0.93 %, peak -5.4 % to +1.8 % (`sensitivity_2023.csv`).
